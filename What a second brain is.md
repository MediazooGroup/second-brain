# What a second brain is

A private, searchable home for your best thinking, in plain files that belong to
you and nobody else.

That is the whole definition. You are looking at one right now.

## Said more plainly

Your mind is for having ideas, not for holding them.

A second brain is the place you put them down so that having the idea and
keeping the idea stop being the same job. It is a folder. It is on your
computer. You can read every file in it with any text editor ever made.

## What it is not

This is the more useful half, because most people give up on the wrong thing.

- **It is not a folder system.** You do not need a structure before you start.
  Structure arrives when you have enough notes to need it, and not before.
  Three notes at the root of a folder is a second brain.
- **It is not an app.** Obsidian is a comfortable window onto plain files. If
  Obsidian vanished tomorrow, your notes are untouched, because they are just
  files.
- **It is not for technical people.** Markdown is ordinary writing plus about
  five small marks. See [[Markdown in five marks]].
- **It is not a filing job.** If keeping it tidy becomes your job, you will quit,
  the way you quit every other system. That is precisely what
  [[Part 3 - hand the tidying over]] is about.
- **It is not about capturing everything.** Capturing everything is how you end
  up with a landfill you are frightened of. Capture the things you would be
  annoyed to lose.

## The three properties that matter

1. **Plain files you own.** No lock-in. Searchable forever. Readable in ten
   years, on a machine that has not been invented yet.
2. **Links, not hierarchy.** A note can sit next to any other note. See
   [[Things that landed]] for the smallest possible example.
3. **A way in.** One page at the front that says what is in here, so you never
   have to hold the whole thing in your head. That is what [[INDEX]] is.

Everything else is decoration.
