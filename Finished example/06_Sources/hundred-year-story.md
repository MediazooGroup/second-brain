# The hundred-year story

The line the intro uses to say "this is not a fad." The urge to keep and connect
your own thinking is a hundred years old and older. AI is just the newest, and
best, tool for an old, human habit.

## The arc, in five stops

1. **Commonplace books (centuries old).** Thinkers, writers and scientists kept
   notebooks of quotes, observations and ideas, cross-referenced by theme. The
   original second brain, on paper.
2. **The memex (1945).** Vannevar Bush imagined a desk that stored all your books
   and notes and let you link them by association — a personal, connected memory
   machine. Decades before the web.
3. **The web and wikis.** Linking ideas together went mainstream, but mostly for
   the world's knowledge, not your own.
4. **Personal knowledge management (2010s).** Tools made it normal to keep your
   own linked notes. The method got a name and a following — but it still made
   maintenance your job.
5. **AI-maintained brains (now).** The assistant reads, files and connects for
   you, and reasons with your material. The maintenance burden that killed every
   previous system finally lifts. This is why now is the moment.

## Why the intro tells it

It reframes the second brain from "another productivity trend" to "the latest
step in something humans have always done" — and lands the punch: the tool
finally caught up with the need.
