# The north star

The one sentence the whole series ladders back to. If you keep nothing else
from four weeks, keep this.

> We consume more than ever, but very little compounds. A second brain makes
> your knowledge and insights accumulate, and in the age of AI, that becomes
> your engine for better thinking.

## Why that word, compound

Compounding is the difference between reading a hundred good things this year
and being any better for it.

Most of what you take in is spent on the day you take it in. You read the
article, you nod, you close the tab. Next month you can remember that there was
an article and roughly that you agreed with it, and that is all you have.

Nothing accumulated. You are back where you started, slightly more tired.

A note is not a better memory. A note is the same thought, still there in
March, ready to be built on by the version of you who knows more.

## What this looks like in a week

- You have a thought in the shower on Tuesday and it still exists on Friday
- Two notes you wrote a month apart turn out to be about the same thing
- Something you half-worked-out in June saves you an afternoon in October
- You stop re-deciding things you already decided

None of that requires a system. It requires the thought to be somewhere.

## The bit that changed recently

This idea is old. People have kept commonplace books for four hundred years and
the good ones worked exactly like this.

What is new is that you can now talk to the pile. A folder of your own writing
is something an AI can read, connect and argue with. That was not possible five
years ago and it is the reason the effort finally pays.
