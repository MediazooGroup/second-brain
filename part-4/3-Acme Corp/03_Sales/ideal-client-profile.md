# Ideal client profile

The organisation we are built for. Used to qualify in at first contact and to qualify out without guilt.

## The one-line version

> A distributed organisation of a thousand people or more, where a named leader owns a change, already reports a number the change should move, and has tried to make it stick once already.

## The five signals

| Signal | What good looks like | How to check it early |
|---|---|---|
| **Scale and spread** | 1,000+ people, working across sites, branches, depots or countries | Ask how many locations, not how many people |
| **A named owner** | One person whose job depends on the change landing, with budget or direct access to it | "Who wakes up worrying about this?" |
| **An existing number** | The department already reports something the change should move | "What do you already measure weekly?" |
| **A live attempt** | They have tried once, and it did not stick | "What did you try last time, and what happened?" |
| **Working tolerance** | Willing to put delegates' real work on the screen | "Would people be able to use their own files in the room?" |

Four out of five is a good client. Three or fewer is a conversation, not an opportunity.

## Strong disqualifiers

- **No number.** If nothing is measured and nothing will be, we do not take the work ([[3-Acme Corp/01_Strategy/strategy-on-a-page|strategy on a page]], non-goals).
- **Procurement-led with no business owner.** A tender with no named leader behind it produces a delivery, not an outcome.
- **A single event wanted.** One awareness day with nothing after it.
- **A data environment nobody will discuss.** If we cannot get a conversation with their security or IT team before contracting, the programme will stall in week four. This is a hard lesson, not a theoretical one.

## Worked example — Parts Unlimited

The account we hold up as the profile made flesh, because it matches four and a half of the five:

- **Scale and spread** — 4,200 people, 60 branches and two distribution centres across the UK and Ireland. Textbook.
- **A named owner** — the aftermarket operations director owns the number and holds the budget; the L&D director sponsors the programme.
- **An existing number** — quote follow-up time is already reported weekly by branch. We did not have to invent a measure.
- **A live attempt** — AI licences were rolled out organisation-wide in January 2026 and adoption stalled at 14% monthly active. That failure is the reason we are in the building.
- **Working tolerance** — yes, with a caveat: customer data cannot leave their environment, which is the constraint the whole programme is designed around. Half a mark rather than a full one.
