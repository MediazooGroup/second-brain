# Sales process

Five stages. A deal moves forward when the **exit condition** is met, not when a date passes.

## The stages

### 1. Conversation

Somebody has a problem and we are talking about it. No proposal, no pricing, no deck.

**Exit condition:** we can name the change, the owner, and the number it should move. If we cannot, we stay here or we stop.

### 2. Qualification

Test the opportunity against [[3-Acme Corp/03_Sales/ideal-client-profile|the ideal client profile]] and get the two conversations that predict everything: the business owner, and the client's IT or security lead.

**Exit condition:** four of five ICP signals, a named business owner, and a security conversation booked or held.

### 3. Shape

Agree the department, the cohorts, the phases and the measure. This is where prioritisation is described but not performed — the prioritisation session itself is inside the engagement, not in front of it.

**Exit condition:** the client has said out loud what number has to move, and how it is currently reported.

### 4. Proposal

Written to the shape already agreed. Nothing in a proposal should be new to the client. Structure and commercial framing in [[3-Acme Corp/03_Sales/commercial-model|the commercial model]].

**Exit condition:** proposal sent and walked through live. Never sent cold.

### 5. Close and handover

Contracting, then the handover to delivery — which is a meeting, not an email. The programme lead joins before signature wherever possible.

**Exit condition:** signed, kick-off dated, and the baseline capture has an owner and a date.

## The rules that keep us honest

- **No proposal before stage 3.** A proposal written to fill a gap in the client's process is a proposal we lose.
- **The security conversation happens in stage 2.** Not stage 4. This is the single most expensive lesson of the last year.
- **We qualify out loud.** If we are going to say no, say it early and say why. Clients refer the suppliers who told them the truth.
- **The measure is a stage gate, not a nice-to-have.** No number, no stage 4.

## What win/loss keeps telling us

- We lose on **speed**, not on price, when the client already has a date in mind and we are still qualifying.
- We win when the **business owner is in the room by the second meeting**. Deals that stay with L&D alone stall at stage 3.
- We lose when we lead with **capability** rather than with the client's own number. The five [[3-Acme Corp/02_Proposition/differentiators|differentiators]] only work after the number is on the table.
