# AI adoption programme

**The flagship.** The offer O1 is written about, and the one most new conversations start with.

## The problem it answers

The organisation has bought AI licences. Somewhere between a tenth and a third of the people who have one open it in a given month, and the ones who do use it as a better search box. Meanwhile the jobs that actually eat the week — drafting the same email, summarising the same call, rewriting the same quote — are still being done by hand.

The gap is not tooling and it is not enthusiasm. It is that nobody has decided **which jobs** are worth handing over, and nobody has shown people how to hand one over properly.

## What the client gets

- A prioritisation session that turns twenty ideas into the three jobs worth changing first.
- A knowledge repository — a second brain — for the department, seeded with the material those three jobs need.
- Two half-days per cohort, in which delegates build working skills against their own files.
- Working artefacts the delegates made themselves, not demo material.
- A manager's guide for the 30 days after, because managers are where new habits go to die.
- A measured outcome, against a baseline captured before the first session.

## How it runs

Four phases, the standard ones in [[3-Acme Corp/05_Product/delivery-model|the delivery model]], with the AI-specific detail:

1. **Discover** — the prioritisation half-day, the security conversation with the client's IT team, and the baseline capture. The baseline is the phase gate: no baseline, no session dates.
2. **Design** — the department's brain structure is agreed, the three jobs are written up as candidate skills, and the sessions are built around them.
3. **Deliver** — two half-days per cohort, a week apart. The week in the middle is not a gap; delegates run their skill on real work and bring back what broke.
4. **Prove** — 30 days later, the measure is taken again and written up with the client.

## Where it goes wrong

- **Too many cohorts too quickly.** Three cohorts is the ceiling before the design needs a review; beyond that, quality drops and nobody notices until the outcome report.
- **The security conversation left late.** If the client's IT team meets us for the first time in week four, we lose two weeks. It is now a Discover-phase task, always.
- **A department that does not own a number.** If the department cannot name something it already reports on, the programme has no measure and we should not have sold it.
- **Skills built on demo data.** A skill that has only ever run on a tidy example folder will fail on the first real one. Delegates use their own files from the first hour.
