# Leadership development

The steady offer. Rarely the first thing a client buys, very often the second.

## The problem it answers

A change has been announced. The senior team believes in it. Six weeks later it has stalled, and when you follow the stall down it always ends in the same place: a middle manager who was told about the change rather than equipped to lead it, and who quietly went back to what they knew.

Managers are not resisting. They have been handed a new expectation with no new practice.

## What the client gets

- Six touchpoints across a quarter for a cohort of managers, not a two-day event.
- A live change of the client's own to practise on — we never invent a case study when the client has a real one.
- A small number of habits, deliberately: hold the conversation, remove one blocker, notice and name progress.
- Peer coaching triads that keep running after we leave, because the cohort has met six times and actually knows each other.

## How it runs

Cohorts of twelve to eighteen, six touchpoints over roughly twelve weeks. Two of the six are half-days, four are ninety-minute working sessions between them. Managers arrive to each session having done something with their own team since the last one, and the session starts with what happened.

## Where it sits in an account

This is the O2 offer. The sequence that works is in [[3-Acme Corp/02_Proposition/offer-catalogue|the offer catalogue]]: prove something in one department with an AI adoption programme, then equip the managers of the next two departments before their cohorts run.

Selling it first is possible but harder, because the client has no proof from us yet and leadership development is the easiest thing in the world to buy from somebody cheaper.

## Where it goes wrong

- **Cohorts built from a list rather than a layer.** A cohort that spans four levels of seniority does not talk honestly.
- **No live change to work on.** Without one, the sessions become interesting and change nothing.
- **The sponsor never appears.** If the senior sponsor does not turn up to at least the first and last touchpoint, the cohort reads the change as optional.
