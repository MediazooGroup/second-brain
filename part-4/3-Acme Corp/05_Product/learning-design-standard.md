# Learning design standard

Every programme is designed backwards from **behaviours**, through **skills**, to **knowledge**. Never the other way round. This is the single standard that makes an Acme programme recognisable from the inside.

## The three layers

**Knowledge** — what someone has to *understand*. Necessary, cheap to deliver, and worth almost nothing on its own. A delegate can hold every fact about a new way of working and change nothing on Monday.

**Skills** — what someone can *do*, unaided, on a real piece of their own work. Demonstrable. If you cannot describe the observable act, it is not a skill; it is knowledge wearing a verb.

**Behaviours** — what someone *keeps doing* when nobody is watching, three weeks later, under time pressure. This is the only layer the client is actually buying.

## The design rule

Write the behaviours first. Then ask, for each behaviour: what must a person be able to *do* to sustain it? Then, for each skill: what is the smallest amount someone must *understand* to perform it?

Anything that survives this walk goes in the programme. Anything that does not is interesting, and gets cut.

## Writing them properly

- **Behaviours are observable and habitual.** "Files the source, not the summary." Not "values good knowledge hygiene."
- **Skills name the artefact.** "Writes a skill file for a repeated job and runs it over a real folder." Not "understands skills."
- **Knowledge is deliberately small.** If the knowledge list is longer than the skills list, the design is wrong.
- **Three to five behaviours per programme.** More than five and none of them land.

## Why it is written this way

Two reasons, both learned the expensive way.

Knowledge is what a client asks for and behaviour is what they are actually paying for. Starting from the request produces a curriculum. Starting from the behaviour produces a change.

And behaviours are measurable in a way knowledge is not. The day-30 measure in [[3-Acme Corp/05_Product/delivery-model|the Prove phase]] is only possible because the behaviours were written down as observable acts at design time. A vague behaviour makes an unmeasurable programme, which makes [[3-Acme Corp/01_Strategy/okrs-2026|KR3.2]] unachievable no matter how good the delivery was.

## Worked example

The clearest current example of the standard applied end to end is [[5-Parts Unlimited Project/02_Delegates/knowledge-skills-behaviours|the Parts Unlimited KSB set]] — three behaviours, four skills, five pieces of knowledge, each mapped to the session that builds it and the evidence that shows it landed.
