# Delivery model — the four phases

Every programme, whichever offer it came from, runs through the same four phases with the same four gates. The content changes; the shape does not.

## Discover

Understand the work before designing anything about it.

- The prioritisation half-day: twenty candidate jobs in, three out, the other seventeen written down in order.
- The security and data conversation with the client's IT team.
- **Baseline capture** — the current value of the number, measured, dated, and signed off by the person who reports it.

**Gate:** no baseline, no session dates. This gate is the whole of the KR3.2 fix and it is not negotiable at delivery level; exceptions go through [[3-Acme Corp/06_Governance/decision-rights|decision rights]].

## Design

Build the programme around the three jobs, not around a curriculum.

- The knowledge structure the work needs — for AI programmes, the department's second brain and its folder shape.
- Each of the three jobs written up as a candidate skill.
- Sessions built backwards from the behaviours in [[3-Acme Corp/05_Product/learning-design-standard|the learning design standard]].

**Gate:** the client's business owner has read the design and can say what their people will be doing differently, in their own words.

## Deliver

Cohorts, in the room or on the call, working on their own files.

- Two half-days per cohort, a week apart, for AI adoption. The week between is part of the design, not a gap.
- Delegates bring back what broke, and session two starts there.
- Facilitator notes go into the project brain the same day, not at the end.

**Gate:** every delegate leaves with at least one working artefact of their own that has run on a real job.

## Prove

The phase everyone skips and the one the whole model is built for.

- The measure taken again, 30 days after close, by the same person who took the baseline.
- The outcome report written **with** the client, not for them.
- The case-study ask made in the same conversation as the outcome report.
- Handover: what the client needs to run the next cohort without us.

**Gate:** the outcome report exists and the client has seen it. A programme is not closed until it does.

## Why the gates matter more than the phases

Any consultancy can list four phases. The difference is that ours stop things. A programme with no baseline does not get dates, and a programme with no outcome report is not closed — which means it does not count as delivered in a review, which means the pressure sits where it belongs.

We were not doing this a year ago. It is why [[3-Acme Corp/01_Strategy/okrs-2026|KR3.2]] is measurable at all, even though it is currently the worst number we have.
