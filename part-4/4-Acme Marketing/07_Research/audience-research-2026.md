# Audience research 2026

Where the personas came from, and the four findings that changed how this department works. Based on **31 buyer conversations** between November 2025 and January 2026, plus the win/loss reviews for the same period. Roles only, no names.

## Finding 1 — we were writing to the wrong person

In 22 of the 31 conversations, the deal did not become real until an operations or commercial leader was in the room. In the nine that never got there, the conversation ended politely at the shaping stage.

Every asset we owned at the time was written to the L&D director.

**What changed:** [[4-Acme Marketing/02_Audience/persona-operations-director|the operations director]] became a first-class persona and half of this year's content is written to them. This is move 3 in [[4-Acme Marketing/01_Strategy/marketing-strategy|the marketing strategy]] and the reason [[4-Acme Marketing/05_Campaigns/campaign-ai-ready-teams|the autumn campaign]] exists in the shape it does.

## Finding 2 — the word "training" costs us the second meeting

Operations leaders consistently reframed the conversation the moment training language appeared. Several said a version of "this is an L&D thing, then" and handed it back.

The same people engaged immediately with "which three jobs eat the most time in your branches?"

**What changed:** in material for that persona, we lead with the job and the number, and the learning vocabulary is removed entirely. See [[4-Acme Marketing/08_Brand/tone-of-voice|tone of voice]].

## Finding 3 — proof beats everything, and we have almost none

"Show me it worked somewhere like us" appeared in 27 of 31 conversations. In the win/loss reviews it was the single most cited reason for choosing another supplier, ahead of price and ahead of scope.

Crucially, the proof that counted was **specific and modest** — one client, one number, one honest caveat — not a page of logos. Two buyers said explicitly that a wall of client logos made them trust a supplier less.

**What changed:** the proof ledger in [[4-Acme Marketing/03_Proposition/proposition-and-proof|proposition and proof]], the rule that we never publish an unmeasured number, and outcome stories becoming play 1.

## Finding 4 — teaching the method openly does not lose us work

We expected "we can do this ourselves" to be triggered by publishing the method. The opposite showed up: the buyers who had read the most of our material were the ones furthest into a conversation, and several said the published method was what made them believe we had actually done it.

The one thing that did feel like giving the game away was the prioritisation framework.

**What changed:** move 2 in the strategy — publish everything except the framework. It is the asymmetry the whole content engine now runs on.

## What we did not find

- No evidence that channel choice matters much. Our buyers are reached by peers and by their own colleagues, not by a platform.
- No evidence that frequency helps. The organisations that engaged had read one or two things properly, not twenty.
- No appetite for gated content of any kind. Two buyers said they would not fill in a form for a supplier they were evaluating.

## When to redo this

The findings are eighteen months old at the point they stop being safe to act on. **Refresh due January 2027**, and earlier if the adoption plateau described in [[3-Acme Corp/07_Research/market-view-ai-skills|the market view]] shifts.
