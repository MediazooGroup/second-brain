# Marketing plays

**Five plays.** A play is a repeatable motion with an owner, a cadence and a job — not a campaign, which is a one-off inside a play. Everything this department does is one of these five, or it needs a reason.

| # | Play | Job | Cadence | Aimed at |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The outcome story** | Proof that travels | Per programme close | Operations director, via the L&D director |
| 2 | **Teach the method live** | Trust at volume | Monthly | L&D director |
| 3 | **The account play** | Depth in the ten accounts that matter | Continuous, with sales | Both buyers, named |
| 4 | **The readiness guide** | Capture and qualify | Always on | L&D director |
| 5 | **Rooms our buyers are already in** | Borrowed audience | Six to eight a year | L&D director, some operations |

## 1. The outcome story

A measured client result, published three ways: a written page with the number in it, a short film of the delegate in their own environment, and a one-page version the client's own comms team can use internally.

**Why it is first:** it is the only asset that reaches [[4-Acme Marketing/02_Audience/persona-operations-director|the operations director]], who does not read anything else we make. It is also the play that produces the **best-qualified conversations by a distance** — inbound off an outcome story arrives with a number already in mind and converts at roughly three times anything else we run. Low volume, highest quality.

**What it depends on:** a measured outcome existing at all, and the client's comms lead saying yes. Both outside our control, which is why the ask now happens in the outcome-report conversation at programme close.

**Owner:** content lead, with the account director. **Status:** one published against a target of three ([[3-Acme Corp/01_Strategy/okrs-2026|KR1.3]]).

## 2. Teach the method live

A monthly live session that teaches part of how the work is actually done — the delivery model, the KSB standard, the folder structures, the failure modes. Recorded, published, no gate.

**Why it works:** it answers "can we do this ourselves?" by saying yes, out loud, and letting the year of production experience be the thing that is hard to copy. It is our highest-volume play and the top of most relationships that end in a deal.

**What we never teach:** the prioritisation framework ([[4-Acme Marketing/01_Strategy/positioning-and-messaging|positioning and messaging]]).

**Owner:** content lead. **Cadence:** monthly, one series per quarter.

## 3. The account play

For the top ten accounts and twenty named targets: a personalised, specific, unsolicited piece of thinking about *their* plateau — what their adoption curve probably looks like, and which three jobs we would look at first. Run jointly with sales, no more than one a fortnight, never templated.

**Why it works:** it is the only play that reliably gets both buyers into the same room, which [[3-Acme Corp/03_Sales/sales-process|the sales process]] says is what wins.

**Owner:** marketing lead with the account directors. **Feeds:** KR2.2.

## 4. The readiness guide

The always-on asset: a practical guide to working out whether an organisation is ready to change how it works, with the four ways an AI programme stalls. Ungated, with a light follow-up for anyone who asks for it.

**Why it works:** it qualifies people *out* as effectively as in, which keeps the pipeline honest. It is the most forwarded thing we have written.

**Owner:** content lead. **Feeds:** campaign [[4-Acme Marketing/05_Campaigns/campaign-ai-ready-teams|AI-ready teams]].

## 5. Rooms our buyers are already in

Speaking slots at L&D and operations community events. We do not sponsor without a slot — that is one of the things we stopped doing this year.

**Owner:** marketing lead. **Cadence:** six to eight a year.

## Which play does the most for the company's objectives

**Play 1, and it is not close.** It owns [[3-Acme Corp/01_Strategy/okrs-2026|KR1.3]] outright, it is the unlock for KR1.1 — because thin proof is what holds conversion back on the flagship offer — and a published outcome in an account is the thing that makes the second programme sellable, which is KR2.3. One play, three key results.

Play 2 produces the most volume and play 3 the highest deal value, but neither moves a company key result on its own.
