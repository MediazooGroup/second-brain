# Content engine

How material actually gets made, so the plays have something to run on. A cadence, not a calendar.

## The principle

**We do not invent content. We harvest it.** Everything worth publishing already happened in a room, a session or a client conversation. The engine's job is to catch it, shape it and get it out — not to sit in front of a blank page inventing opinions.

This is why the engine is wired into delivery rather than sitting beside it.

## The four sources

| Source | What it produces | Who catches it |
|---|---|---|
| **Programme close** | Outcome stories, delegate films, the honest failure notes | Content lead, in the outcome-report conversation |
| **Live sessions** | The teaching material for play 2, and every question the audience asks | Content lead, same week |
| **Client conversations** | The objections, the language, the plateau numbers | Whoever was in the room, into the inbox |
| **The method itself** | Delivery model, KSB standard, folder structures, failure modes | Content lead, from the org brain |

Anything caught but not yet filed goes into `00_Inbox` and gets triaged weekly — see [[4-Acme Marketing/06_Governance/ways-of-working|ways of working]].

## The weekly rhythm

- **Monday** — inbox triage. Everything captured last week gets filed, turned into a draft, or deleted. Nothing sits for a fortnight.
- **Tuesday** — one long-form piece drafted, written to one persona.
- **Thursday** — review against [[4-Acme Marketing/08_Brand/tone-of-voice|tone of voice]] and [[4-Acme Marketing/03_Proposition/proposition-and-proof|the proof ledger]]. The reviewer checks every number against its source note.
- **Friday** — publish, and the derivatives go out over the following week.

## One piece, five uses

A single outcome story becomes: the written page, the delegate film, a one-page internal version for the client's comms team, two LinkedIn posts written to different personas, and a section of the next live session. **We plan the derivatives before we make the original**, because retro-fitting them never happens.

## The review gate

Nothing publishes without a second pair of eyes checking three things:

1. Which persona is this written to, and would they read past the first line?
2. Does every claim have a source note linked?
3. Is there an honest bit in it?

If a draft fails any of the three, it goes back. This is the only formal gate in the department and it exists because our whole positioning rests on being believable.
