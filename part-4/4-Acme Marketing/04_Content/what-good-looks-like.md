# What good looks like

The standards, shown rather than stated. Four formats, with the opening that works and the opening that does not.

## The outcome story

The example below is a **shape, not a published claim.** It is drafted from the programme currently in delivery, with the numbers left as blanks, and it does not go anywhere until the measure lands on 30 October and the client has approved the words. We have exactly one published outcome story — see [[4-Acme Marketing/03_Proposition/proposition-and-proof|the proof ledger]] — and drafting the second early is allowed. Publishing it early is not.

**Good, first two lines:**

> Quote follow-up at a 60-branch parts distributor took [BASELINE] minutes. [N] branch managers got it to [RESULT], and it was still there thirty days later.

**Bad:**

> Acme Corp is delighted to announce the successful completion of a transformative AI enablement programme with a leading automotive parts distributor.

**Why:** the good one opens on the reader's number and survives being read by someone who has never heard of us. The bad one is about us, contains no information, and would be scrolled past by every one of our three personas.

**The rule:** the number and the constraint in the first two lines. Our name no earlier than the third paragraph. The honest bit before the end. **No number goes in until it has been measured**, and no client is named until they have approved that use in writing.

## The LinkedIn post

**Good:**

> Every organisation I've walked into this year bought the licences before deciding which jobs would change. We asked 141 people last week what share of licence holders use it in a normal week: 58% said under a quarter. It isn't scepticism — people tried it twice on something it was bad at and went back to their week.

**Bad:**

> AI adoption is the biggest challenge facing L&D leaders today. 🧵 Here's what most people get wrong…

**Why:** the good one is a specific observation from real work with a real number in it. The bad one is a hook with no content, and it makes a claim about "most people" that we cannot source.

**The rule:** one observation, from something that actually happened, written to one persona. No hook questions. No emoji threads.

## The account note

**Good:** opens with something true about *their* organisation that we worked out ourselves — the shape of their branch network, the fact that their licences went out in January, the number they already report weekly.

**Bad:** anything that could be sent to a different company with the name swapped.

**The rule:** if it survives find-and-replace, it is not an account note. One a fortnight, never templated.

## The live session

**Good:** opens with a failure — the way this goes wrong, and what it cost. Then the method. Then the questions, which are the most valuable twenty minutes of the hour and get captured straight into the inbox.

**Bad:** opens with an agenda slide and a company overview.

**The rule:** teach something genuinely useful within four minutes, or the audience is gone.

## The universal tells of a bad draft

- The first line is about Acme.
- A number with no source note linked.
- No honest bit anywhere in it.
- Written to "L&D and operations leaders" — two personas at once, therefore nobody.
- An adjective doing the work a specific would have done better.
