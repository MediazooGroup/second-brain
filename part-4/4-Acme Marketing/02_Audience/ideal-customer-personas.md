# Ideal customer personas

**Three personas.** One buys the programme, one owns the number that justifies it, and one decides whether the rest of the organisation ever hears about it. They sit inside the company-level [[3-Acme Corp/03_Sales/ideal-client-profile|ideal client profile]] — that note describes the *organisation*, this one describes the *people* in it.

## The three, in one line each

| Persona | Role in the buy | The sentence they say |
|---|---|---|
| [[4-Acme Marketing/02_Audience/persona-ld-director\|The L&D director]] | **Buyer.** Owns the budget and the programme | "We've done the training. It didn't stick." |
| [[4-Acme Marketing/02_Audience/persona-operations-director\|The operations director]] | **Economic owner.** Owns the number the change should move | "Show me it worked somewhere like us." |
| [[4-Acme Marketing/02_Audience/persona-internal-comms-lead\|The internal comms lead]] | **Amplifier and gatekeeper.** Decides what the organisation hears | "Nobody's going to read another all-staff email." |

## What each one actually worries about

**The L&D director** worries about being seen as the department that runs courses. They have a budget, a reputation for delivery, and no way to prove impact — so every year their spend is defended on volume rather than value. Their private fear is that AI makes their function look slow and irrelevant while they are still doing the annual compliance cycle.

**The operations director** worries about a number that is going the wrong way, this quarter. They are not interested in learning, capability or engagement, and they will say so. Their fear is disruption: pulling people off the floor for training that costs them a week of capacity and returns nothing. They are the hardest of the three to reach and the fastest to buy once convinced, because they can attach the spend to something they already report.

**The internal comms lead** worries about credibility. They have announced things before that quietly did not happen, and each one costs them audience. Their fear is being asked to publicise a change that will not survive, because they are the one whose name is on the email.

## How they interact

The L&D director opens the conversation. The operations director decides whether it is real. The internal comms lead determines whether anyone else in the organisation ever knows it happened — which is where our next case study comes from.

Deals that stay with the L&D director alone stall at the shaping stage. That pattern is confirmed in [[3-Acme Corp/03_Sales/sales-process|the sales process]]: we win when the business owner is in the room by the second meeting.

## Who is not a persona

- **IT and security.** Critical to the delivery, never the buyer. They are a constraint to design around, not an audience to market to.
- **The CEO.** Sponsors sometimes, buys almost never.
- **The delegates themselves.** They are the audience for the *programme*, not for our marketing.

## What this changes in our work

Half of this year's content is written to the operations director, who we historically ignored — move 3 in [[4-Acme Marketing/01_Strategy/marketing-strategy|the marketing strategy]]. The practical test on any draft: **which of the three would read past the first line?** If the answer is "all of them", it is written to nobody.
