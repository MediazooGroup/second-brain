# Persona — the L&D director

**The buyer.** Owns the budget, owns the programme, and carries the reputation of the function.

## Who they are

Runs learning and development for an organisation of a few thousand people spread across sites. A team of four to twelve. Reports into HR, occasionally into transformation. Has been in the role three to six years and in the profession much longer.

Competent, busy, and quietly frustrated. They know their function is judged on delivery volume rather than on anything that matters, and they have not found the lever to change that.

## What they are trying to do this year

- Get something onto the executive agenda that is not compliance.
- Show impact in a language the business recognises, which is not completion rates.
- Do something credible about AI before someone else in the organisation does it for them.
- Stop paying for programmes that evaporate four weeks after launch.

## What they say

> "We've done the training. It didn't stick."

> "I need to be able to show the exec something more than attendance."

> "Everyone's talking about AI. I don't want to run an awareness webinar and call it a strategy."

## What they actually fear

That L&D is the department that gets things done rather than the department that changes things — and that AI is the moment where that becomes obvious to everyone.

## What makes them trust us

- We talk about the four weeks *after* the training, unprompted, before they raise it.
- We agree the measure with the business, which gives them a number to take upstairs.
- We say what did not work in a previous programme. Suppliers who only tell them about successes get discounted immediately.
- We hand over the method, so they are not buying a dependency.

## What loses them

- Learning jargon. They have heard all of it and use it defensively, not affectionately.
- A curriculum-shaped proposal. It confirms exactly the thing they are trying to escape.
- Anything that sounds like it will need the business's time without the business's agreement.

## Where they are

L&D professional communities and their peer network, far more than any channel we control. They read long-form if a peer sent it. They attend the sessions their peers attend. They almost never respond to cold outreach and reliably respond to a specific, useful thing sent without an ask.

## What to send them

- The outcome story, with the number and the honest bit included.
- The live teaching series, because it lets them evaluate us with no commitment.
- The failure modes: the four ways an AI programme stalls. It is the most forwarded thing we have written.
