# Persona — the internal comms lead

**The amplifier, and the gatekeeper.** Rarely signs anything. Determines whether the rest of the organisation ever hears that the change happened — which is where our next case study comes from.

## Who they are

Owns internal communications for the organisation. A small team, often one or two people, sitting in HR, corporate affairs or the transformation office. Writes everything the workforce reads from the centre, and is held responsible for whether people "engaged" with it.

## What they are trying to do

- Get people to believe an internal message again, after several that did not survive contact with reality.
- Find real stories from the front line instead of writing another leadership quote.
- Prove that comms did something, using a measure that is not open rate.

## What they say

> "Nobody's going to read another all-staff email."

> "Have you got anything from someone who actually does the job?"

> "I'm not announcing this until I know it's real."

## What they actually fear

Being the name at the bottom of an email about something that then quietly dies. Every one of those costs them audience, and audience is the only asset they have.

## Why they matter to us

Three reasons, in order:

1. **They are the cheapest way into a department that has bought nothing.** Comms teams talk to everybody and are usually glad of a supplier who brings them material rather than asking for it.
2. **They unblock our biggest constraint.** [[3-Acme Corp/01_Strategy/okrs-2026|KR1.3]] needs published outcome stories, and the client-side person who can actually get one approved and filmed is almost always this one.
3. **They are the internal advocate for the story travelling.** A delegate film they helped make gets shown at their all-hands, which is how the next department hears about us.

## What makes them trust us

- We bring them a story, already shaped, with the delegate's permission already asked.
- We write in the delegate's voice rather than in ours.
- We are honest about what did not work, which is the thing that makes an internal story survive scrutiny.

## What loses them

- Marketing material with our logo on it, offered as internal comms. It is instantly recognisable and instantly refused.
- Asking for a case study before the outcome exists.
- Anything that makes their leadership look better than the evidence supports.

## What to send them

- The delegate film, cut for internal use, with our branding off it.
- A short "how to tell this story internally" guide — the most useful thing we give away for free.
- Never a proposal.
