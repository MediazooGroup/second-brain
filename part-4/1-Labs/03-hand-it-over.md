# Hand it over

![[1-Labs/images/03-hand-it-over.png]]

Here is where most people stop. They believe in the idea, they understand the files, and then they look at the volume of material they would have to organise — and quietly never start.

So you do not do the filing. You write down how the filing should be done, once, and hand that over.

That is a skill. A plain file, `SKILL.md`, holding a process you repeat: when it runs, what it needs from you, what it will never do, and what you get back. Both of the ones we shipped are sitting in this vault:

[[2-Skills/second-brain-builder/SKILL]]

[[2-Skills/second-brain-updater/SKILL]]

One builds a brain from a pile of source material. One keeps it current when something new lands. Both show you the plan **before** they write anything, and neither touches a file until you type the exact approval phrase.

That is the loop, and it is four steps:

1. Drop a source in — a transcript, a brief, a voice memo, an email worth keeping
2. The skill proposes what it would change
3. You say yes, no, or edit
4. The brain is stronger than it was

Part three, in full: `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WuP79sQ0O9s`

**Those same two skills run against every brain you are about to see.** Nothing new is needed to go from one person to a whole business. That is the point of today.
