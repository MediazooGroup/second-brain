# The departmental brain

![[1-Labs/images/05-the-departmental-brain.png]]

Your best marketer hands in their notice on a Friday.

You have four weeks. In those four weeks you will discover how much of that department was never written down anywhere: which campaigns actually worked and why, what tone the emails are supposed to be in, who the content is for, which of the five things they run is the one that matters.

They will write a handover document. It will be four pages long. It will not be enough, and everyone knows it.

**A departmental brain is that handover document, written continuously, before anybody leaves.**

## The distinction that matters

The organisational brain we just looked at standardises **what is true**. A departmental brain does something different: it makes a department **depend on process rather than on people**.

Marketing does not stop because someone is on holiday. Sales does not forget how it qualifies. Finance does not lose the reason a report is shaped the way it is. The department runs on written process, and the people bring the judgement.

That is the job. It is not documentation for its own sake.

## Read it the way a new starter would

Forget the folder numbers for a moment. Imagine you joined this department this morning.

You would read [[4-Acme Marketing/06_Governance/ways-of-working|ways of working]] — who does what, what happens on a Monday, and the test the department holds itself to: *if the marketing lead is away for a month, does anything stop?*

Then you would read the person you are writing to this week. Not "our audience" — a specific one of [[4-Acme Marketing/02_Audience/ideal-customer-personas|three personas]], each with what they actually worry about at two in the morning. The buyer. The person who owns the number. The person who decides whether anyone else ever hears about it.

Then you would look at what the department runs.

## The five plays

This is the page I would show anyone who thinks a departmental brain is filing. [[4-Acme Marketing/05_Campaigns/marketing-plays|Marketing plays]] — five repeatable motions, each with an owner and a cadence.

| Play | Job | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| The outcome story | Proof that travels | Per programme close |
| Teach the method live | Trust at volume | Monthly |
| The account play | Depth in ten accounts | Continuous, with sales |
| The readiness guide | Capture and qualify | Always on |
| Rooms our buyers are in | Borrowed audience | Six to eight a year |

Five things. Not a calendar, not a wish list. And the note says plainly which one moves the company's objectives most, and why — which means a new starter can be usefully wrong on their second day instead of their second month.

Notice something else about that page. It links **upward**, to the company's OKRs. The department knows what it is accountable for, in the organisation's own words, without restating them and letting the two drift apart.

## And it has an inbox

Open [[4-Acme Marketing/00_Inbox/2026-08-17-webinar-poll-results|the inbox]]. That is raw. Poll results and audience questions from a session two days ago, dropped in unsorted, with a note at the bottom saying what it might mean and what to raise on Monday.

It is not filed yet, and that is correct. **A department that has nowhere to put a half-formed thought loses the half-formed thoughts.** They are usually the good ones. That page has a real insight in it — the audience confirmed a number the department had only assumed — and in a fortnight it will have changed a live campaign.

## Where it goes wrong

**It becomes a copy of the org brain.** Same folders, same content, said twice, and now you maintain both. If a department's brain restates the mission, delete it and link up.

**It becomes an archive.** Every campaign ever run, kept forever, with no signal about what worked. The plays page is the antidote: five things, not fifty.

**It is written by one person for themselves.** Then it is not a departmental brain, it is a diary — and you are back to the four-page handover.

**It has no rhythm.** Notice how much of Acme's marketing brain is about *when*: inbox triage on Monday, content on Tuesday, review on Thursday, plays reviewed monthly. A brain with no rhythm is a snapshot.

## Takeaway

An organisational brain standardises the organisation. **A departmental brain makes the department depend on process, not people.**

Same files, same markdown, same two skills. Different job.

## Ask it yourself

Point Cowork at `4-Acme Marketing` and go one at a time.

1. Who are our ideal customer personas, and what does each of them actually worry about?
2. What are our key marketing plays, and which one produces the best-qualified conversations?
3. What are our brand guidelines — colour, type, the logo rules, and the things we never do?
4. An L&D director has just read our readiness guide. What do we send them next, and why that?
5. Which marketing play does the most for the company's objectives this year, and which key result does it own?
6. Write a LinkedIn post for the operations director persona, in our tone of voice, about the thing they complain about most.
7. What did our audience research change about the way this department works?
