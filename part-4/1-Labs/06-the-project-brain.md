# The project brain

![[1-Labs/images/06-the-project-brain.png]]

Six weeks out from a client deadline, someone asks a simple question: *why did we decide to split the cohorts that way?*

Nobody can find it. It was agreed on a call. It is in a chat thread, or an email, or in the head of the person who is on annual leave. So it gets discussed again, from scratch, and this time a different answer wins — and now two people are working to two different plans.

**That is the failure a project brain prevents. Not filing. Re-litigation.**

## What makes this lens different

The other two brains are permanent. This one has an end date.

A project brain is **time-boxed, deadline-shaped and disposable**. It exists to get a deliverable over a line, and when the line is crossed it stops. Everything in it is one of three things: something we promised, someone we promised it to, or a record of what happened.

Which is why its shape is nothing like the other two. Here it is:

[[5-Parts Unlimited Project/INDEX]]

Acme Corp is delivering AI training to a client called Parts Unlimited. 48 people, 60 branches, three cohorts, and a deadline of the thirtieth of September.

## The four things it must always answer

Any project brain, in any industry. If it cannot answer these four, it is not doing its job.

**What are we delivering?** [[5-Parts Unlimited Project/03_Deliverables/deliverables|Six deliverables]], each with an owner, a date and a definition of done. Alongside them, written down in the [[5-Parts Unlimited Project/01_Brief/scope-and-success|scope]], the list of what is explicitly **out** — so that the question stops being asked.

**Who is it for?** [[5-Parts Unlimited Project/02_Delegates/who-is-in-the-room|48 delegates]], four roles, three cohorts, and the thing the client cares about most: exactly what it costs them in hours off the shop floor.

**What has to change?** [[5-Parts Unlimited Project/02_Delegates/knowledge-skills-behaviours|Knowledge, skills and behaviours]] — three behaviours, four skills, five pieces of knowledge, each mapped to the session that builds it and the evidence that shows it landed. Written backwards, behaviours first, because behaviour is the only layer the client is actually buying.

**When is it due?** [[5-Parts Unlimited Project/04_Runway/milestones-and-deadline|The runway]].

## Read the runway

This is the page that makes a project brain feel different from everything else you have seen today.

Every date, in order, from contract signature to final report. Two of them in bold, because they are the only two things that can move the deadline — a security sign-off due on the twenty-sixth of August, and a baseline measurement due on the twenty-eighth.

And then the sentence that earns the whole page: **after the first of September there is no slack whatsoever.**

Read the deadline entry carefully, because it contains the trap. The thirtieth of September is when the *interim* report is due. The full measured result cannot land until the thirtieth of October, because the last cohort finishes on the twenty-ninth of September and thirty days is arithmetic, not negotiation.

Everyone on that programme now knows the difference. Before it was written down, half of them did not.

## The log is where the value hides

Three files, and they are the least glamorous pages in the vault.

[[5-Parts Unlimited Project/06_Log/decisions|Decisions]] — eight entries, each with a line saying what it **closes off**. That is the trick. "Phase one is aftermarket only" closes off "could we just add the distribution centres?" — a question that has been asked twice since and answered from the log both times without a meeting.

[[5-Parts Unlimited Project/06_Log/risks-and-issues|Risks and issues]] — ranked, owned, dated. The biggest exposure on that programme is not a delivery risk at all. It is that everyone declares victory at the conference on the eighth of October and the real measurement never happens.

[[5-Parts Unlimited Project/06_Log/status|Status]] — one page, every Monday, amber, and why.

## What happens at the end

The bit people skip.

On the thirtieth of October this brain stops being a working brain. Three things happen to it. Anything general — what we learned about this client, what we would change in the design — gets **promoted up** into the organisational brain. The outcome story goes to **marketing**, which has been waiting for it all year. And the rest is **archived**.

That is the whole system working. A project feeds the organisation. The organisation is what the next project starts from.

## Where it goes wrong

**It is shaped like the org brain.** Numbered folders full of strategy, and no runway. A project brain that cannot tell you what is due next Tuesday is decoration.

**It is only ever written after the fact.** Meeting notes typed up on the last day of the month are fiction. The rule on this one: facilitator notes go in the same day.

**Nothing gets promoted at the end.** The programme finishes, the folder is archived, and everything learned goes with it. Then the next project starts from nothing, again.

**It becomes the project's only reporting.** It is not a substitute for talking to the client. It is what makes the conversation short.

## Takeaway

Four lenses, one habit.

**Personal** — so your own thinking compounds. **Organisational** — to standardise what is true. **Departmental** — so a department runs on process, not people. **Project** — to get a deliverable over a line, then feed what it learned back up.

Same plain files. Same markdown. The same two skills from part three run against every one of them.

## Ask it yourself

Point Cowork at `5-Parts Unlimited Project`.

1. What are we delivering to Parts Unlimited, and what is explicitly out of scope?
2. Who are the delegates — how many, which roles, and how are the cohorts split?
3. What knowledge, skills and behaviours is this programme meant to change, and how will we know it worked?
4. When is the deadline, what exactly is due on it, and what is most likely to make us miss it?
5. What have we already decided that I should not reopen?
6. Draft the joining instructions for cohort one, and make sure they follow Acme's brand and voice rules.
