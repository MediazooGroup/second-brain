# The organisational brain

![[1-Labs/images/04-the-organisational-brain.png]]

Ask five people in your company what the business is trying to do this year. You will get five answers. Not because anyone is wrong — because each of them last saw it on a different slide, in a different meeting, six months apart.

Now imagine handing your AI assistant that same mess. It has nothing to stand on either. So it does what a new starter does: it makes something plausible up.

**That is what an organisational brain fixes. One answer, in one place, that a person and a machine both read.**

## What it is

A folder of plain files holding what is true **across the whole company**. Not department detail, not project detail. The things that outlive any one engagement: what we believe, what we sell, how we win, how we deliver, how we sound.

We have built one for a fictional company for today. Acme Corp — a training and consultancy business, 140 people. It is here, in the vault, and everything I am about to say is checkable:

[[3-Acme Corp/INDEX]]

## Walk its shape

Open the index and look at the folder numbers down the left. This is the whole company in eight drawers.

**01_Strategy** — why we exist and what we are trying to do this year. Three files. The [[3-Acme Corp/01_Strategy/mission|mission]], the [[3-Acme Corp/01_Strategy/okrs-2026|OKRs]], and the [[3-Acme Corp/01_Strategy/strategy-on-a-page|strategy on a page]]. That last one has a section most companies never write down: what we deliberately do **not** do.

**02_Proposition** — what we sell, and why anyone picks us. The three offers, and the [[3-Acme Corp/02_Proposition/differentiators|differentiators]] written as answers to objections, because that is the order they get used in.

**03_Sales** — who we sell to and how. Including the [[3-Acme Corp/03_Sales/ideal-client-profile|ideal client profile]], which is as much about who to say no to.

**04_Marketing** — one file. Deliberately. It says what marketing is accountable for and then points down at the department's own brain.

**05_Product** — how we design and deliver. The [[3-Acme Corp/05_Product/delivery-model|delivery model]] and the [[3-Acme Corp/05_Product/learning-design-standard|learning design standard]].

**06_Governance** — how decisions get made, and how the brain itself is maintained.

**07_Research** — what we have learned about our market and our buyers.

**08_Brand** — how we look and how we sound.

Seventy or so pages across three brains, and none of it is a database. It is text files in folders.

## The thing that makes it work

Look at any note and you will see it links to others rather than repeating them. The account page does not restate the mission. The offer does not re-explain the delivery model.

**A fact written down twice will disagree with itself within a quarter.** So it gets written once and linked to. That single discipline is most of the difference between a brain and a shared drive.

## Who actually touches it

Not everyone, and not often. That is fine.

Someone writing a proposal opens the differentiators. Someone new opens the index on day one and gets the company in twenty minutes instead of six weeks. Someone in a pitch checks what we are allowed to claim. And an assistant reads all of it, every time anybody asks it anything.

The value is not that people browse it. It is that **the answer exists and can be checked.**

## Where it goes wrong

**It becomes a dumping ground.** Everything gets added, nothing is ever removed, and within a year nobody trusts it. Acme's [[3-Acme Corp/06_Governance/ways-of-working|ways of working]] has a pruning rhythm in it for exactly this reason. A brain grows by deleting as much as by writing.

**It holds the wrong things.** Prices, personal data, live project noise. Look at Acme's commercial model — it describes how a deal is shaped and deliberately carries no figures. Numbers belong in a system, not in a brain.

**It gets written for the machine.** Bullet fragments, tables of keywords, no prose. Then a human opens it, finds it unreadable, and stops maintaining it. Write it for a colleague. The machine copes fine.

**Nobody owns it.** A brain with no maintenance rhythm is a snapshot of the day it was made.

## Takeaway

An organisational brain **standardises the organisation**. One version of what is true, that a person can read and a machine can cite.

That is this lens and only this lens. The next one is a different job entirely.

## Ask it yourself

Point Cowork at `3-Acme Corp` and ask these, one at a time. Watch which files each answer names.

1. What is Acme Corp's mission, and how would you say it to a client in one sentence?
2. What are our OKRs this year, and which key result is furthest behind?
3. Which of our three offers is doing the most work against the AI adoption objective, and why?
4. A prospect says "we could do this ourselves." What does the brain give me to answer that?
5. What are we deliberately not doing this year, and why?
6. Who is our ideal client, and which parts of that profile does Parts Unlimited actually match?
7. Draft the opening paragraph of a proposal, in our voice, for a company whose managers are not using the AI licences they bought.
