# Point an AI at this folder

Once you have a folder of notes, you can point an AI at it and talk to it. Not
about the world in general. About your own writing.

That is the moment the whole thing stops being filing and starts being useful.

You can do this in about ten minutes, this afternoon, with no technical
background at all.

## The one app

**Claude Desktop.** It is free to start, you point it at a folder on your own
computer, and you ask it questions about what is in there.

There are other ways to do this. This is one path, chosen so you are not stood
in front of a menu. If you already use something else and it can read a local
folder, use that instead. Nothing here depends on the brand.

One honest aside, because you will hear it mentioned: the version John runs is
**Cowork**, which is the same company's version built for teams working
together. You do not need it for any of this. Claude Desktop and the folder you
just made is the whole thing.

## The steps

1. Go to `https://claude.ai/download` and download **Claude for desktop**. Pick
   your machine, Mac or Windows.
2. Install it, open it, and sign in. Creating an account is free.
3. Put this folder somewhere you can find it. Your Desktop is ideal. If you
   still have it in your Downloads folder, move it now, before you point
   anything at it.
4. In Claude Desktop, open **Settings**. On a Mac it is in the Claude menu at
   the top of the screen. On Windows it is in the menu inside the app window.
5. Find **Extensions**. Extensions are small add-ons that let the app do things
   beyond chatting.
6. Add the one called **Filesystem**. That is the one that gives it permission
   to read files on your computer. Install it.
7. It will ask which folders it is allowed to see. Choose this folder, and only
   this folder. It cannot read anything you do not point it at.
8. Close settings and start a new conversation.
9. Ask it something. Start with this, typed exactly:

   > Read the INDEX file in the Second brain starter folder and tell me what is
   > in there.

If it comes back describing your notes, you are connected. That is it. That is
the whole setup.

The wording of the buttons moves around a little between versions. If a label
does not match, look for the nearest thing to it. The shape is always the same:
settings, then extensions, then Filesystem, then choose the folder.

## Three things to ask it once it works

Try these in order. They get better as they go.

1. **Find something.** *"Which of my notes mention front matter, and what does
   each one say about it?"* This is search, but it reads the notes rather than
   matching a word.

2. **Connect two things.** *"My note on what makes our content land and my note on
   things that landed. What is the argument running through both of them?"*
   This is the one that surprises people. It is not retrieving. It is reading
   your two notes together and telling you what you were getting at.

3. **Make something new from what is already there.** *"Using only what is in
   these notes, write me a short summary of what the four week programme is
   about, for a colleague who has not seen it."* Only what is in these notes.
   That constraint is doing a lot of work, and it is the habit worth keeping.

Then ask it about your own notes, not these ones. That is when it becomes
yours.

## What it can do

- Read every note in the folder, including the front matter at the top.
- Answer questions about what you have written, in your own words, with the
  note it came from.
- Find the connection between two notes you had not put together.
- Draft something new from your material: a summary, an outline, a first pass
  at a post.
- Suggest where a new note should live and what should link to it.

## What it cannot do, and you should know this before you rely on it

- **It only knows what you have written down.** It is not clever about your
  work. It is reading your notes. A thin note gets you a thin answer, and the
  fix is always to write more down, not to ask better.
- **It can be confidently wrong.** It will occasionally state something as fact
  that is not in your notes at all. If an answer matters, open the note it came
  from and check. Ask it to quote the note back to you.
- **It only sees the folders you point it at.** That is a feature. It also means
  a note you keep somewhere else may as well not exist.
- **It does not tidy up on its own.** It will not file, rename or rewrite
  anything unless you ask it to, and when you do ask, read the change before you
  accept it. Your notes are the asset. Everything else is replaceable.
- **It needs the internet.** Obsidian works on a plane. This does not.
- **Free accounts have usage limits.** You will hit a pause if you use it hard
  in one sitting. It resets.

## The one thing to be careful about

When you ask a question about your notes, the notes it needs go off your
computer to the service to be read. That is how it works, and it is worth
knowing before you point it at anything.

So use your judgement about the folder you choose. If it holds something you
would not put in an email, keep that in a different folder and point the AI at
this one.
